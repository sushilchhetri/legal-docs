Privacy Policy Galaxy Hotspot Chat

Effective Date: October 2026

Galaxy Hotspot Chat is an offline chat app made by The Sushi Apps. It lets phones that are near each other chat over the same Wi‑Fi, a phone's hotspot, Wi‑Fi Direct or Bluetooth, without internet. This Privacy Policy explains what information the app handles. In short: there are no accounts, and your chats never go through our servers. We don't have servers that receive them.

1. Your Messages & Media

Chats travel directly between the phones in a room. Text, photos, videos, voice messages, files, stickers, polls, reactions and shared locations are sent from your phone to the room's host phone, which passes them on to the other people in the room. Nothing is sent to us or stored on any server.

Encryption: Between phones running this version of the app, every connection is encrypted (AES‑256‑GCM, with keys agreed by the two phones for that connection). If the host sets a room password, it is also needed to make those keys, so people without the password cannot read the room. The host's phone decrypts each message to pass it on, as any member of the room can read it. Phones running older versions of the app join rooms using the older, unencrypted format.

Private messages inside a room are delivered only to the person you choose (through the host's phone). Delete for everyone asks the other phones to remove a message, but it cannot undo copies someone has already saved, exported or received on an older app version.

Saved on your device: your chat history and the media you send and receive are stored only in the app on your phone. You can search it, export a chat as a text file (shared only where you choose), or delete it at any time by swiping it away in Chat History. Everything is removed when you uninstall the app.

2. Your Profile

You choose a display name and, if you like, a profile photo. Both are stored on your device and shared only with the people in the rooms you host or join, so they can see who is talking. They are not uploaded to us. You can use a different name for any room.

When you host a room, people on the same network can see its name, your display name, how many people are in it and whether it is locked, so they can find it. Wi‑Fi Direct rooms also share their Wi‑Fi network name and password with nearby phones so they can connect; a room password still keeps the chat itself private.

3. Permissions We Ask For

  • Camera: only when you take a photo to send or to use as your profile photo.
  • Microphone: only while you hold the button to record a voice message.
  • Location: only when you tap Share location; your position is sent to that room and nowhere else. On older Android versions, Android also requires this permission to use Wi‑Fi Direct and Bluetooth; the app does not use it to find where you are.
  • Nearby devices (Wi‑Fi Direct and Bluetooth): to create and find Wi‑Fi Direct rooms and to connect to phones over Bluetooth. Not used to work out your location. When you let Bluetooth guests join, Android may ask to make your phone visible to nearby devices for a few minutes.
  • Notifications: to show new messages and that a room is running while the app is in the background.
  • Wi‑Fi and network access: to connect phones on the same Wi‑Fi or hotspot and to find rooms on that network.
  • Photos, videos and files: the app uses Android's picker, so it only sees the items you choose to send. It does not ask for access to all your storage.

You can turn any permission off in your phone's settings; only the feature that needs it stops working.

4. Information Collected Automatically

When your phone is online, the app uses a few Google Firebase services to keep the app working well. None of them receive your messages, media, contacts or profile.

  • Firebase Analytics: general usage information such as which screens are used, app version, device model, Android version, language and approximate region (worked out from your IP address), linked to a random app‑instance ID. The app does not use your advertising ID.
  • Firebase Crashlytics: if the app crashes, a crash report with technical details (what went wrong in the code, device model, Android version, app version and free memory/storage) so we can fix it.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging: a notification token so we can send occasional app announcements.

The app shows no ads.

5. Third-Party Services

These providers process data under their own privacy policies:

  • Google Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics, Cloud Messaging): Firebase Privacy & Security and the Google Privacy Policy
  • Google Play (app downloads and updates)

We do not sell or share your personal information for advertising.

6. Data Retention & Deletion

  • Chats, media and your profile stay on your device until you delete them or uninstall the app. Copies that other people received stay on their phones.
  • Analytics and crash data are kept by Google for a limited time, as described in Firebase's documentation, and are not tied to your name or messages.

7. Your Choices & Rights

  • Delete any saved chat in Chat History by swiping it away.
  • Change or remove your name and photo in Settings → Edit profile.
  • Turn permissions off at any time in your phone's settings.
  • Remove all app data by uninstalling the app.
  • Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or CCPA), you may have the right to access or delete personal data. Because we keep no account or chat data, most requests concern Firebase analytics and crash data; contact us and we will help.

8. Security

Connections between phones on the current version are encrypted, and room passwords are never stored or sent in readable form. Still, no method of sending or storing data is perfectly secure, and anyone in a room can read, save or forward what is shared there, so only share what you are comfortable sharing with the people in that room.

9. Children's Privacy

Galaxy Hotspot Chat is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.
